# About the author

<div class="cp-bubble">It looks like you want to know who's responsible for this. Fair. He'd like you to know it was on purpose.</div>

**Adam Matthew Steinberger** is a staff software engineer in Greenville, South Carolina. He builds AI platforms for places where security and compliance are the requirements: identity governance kept in Git, LLM gateways with spend caps and tamper-evident audit trails, and release pipelines that hold up to review. He has spent 13+ years shipping production systems in insurance, fintech, healthcare, and cybersecurity. He's also an autistic engineer who says so in his headline because it's part of how he works: systematically, thoroughly, and with a preference for evidence over adjectives. That's the sensibility behind a paperclip pet that ships with blind QA reports.

His larger open-source project is [vibey](https://github.com/adammatthewsteinberger/vibey), a conductor that carries a change from spec through design, build, and review across several coding agents. Every decision lands in an append-only ledger, and a person is called in only at recorded approval gates.

He writes, too. *Engineering Influence* is a 200-plus-source field manual on how influence, attention, and culture actually work — hooks that deliver what they promise, awe over outrage, and a hard rule against manipulating the audience you're trying to serve. This documentation site is an attempt to practise the honest half of that on a small, silly, well-made thing. His other book, *Novice to Navigator*, is a plain-language guide to AI chatbots for business; the first edition is free to read on his site.

He builds and maintains privacy-first, open-source software, prefers tools that don't phone home, and thinks a good disclaimer is a feature.
